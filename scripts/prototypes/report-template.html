Does Vegas disagree with the model?

Polymarket runs a season-long "who leads fantasy points at each position" market for 2026–27 — a live field of real-money probabilities, priced months before Week 1. This reallocates the same JP $ pool our bottoms-up model already assigns to each position, in the market's proportions instead of VORP, to see where real money and the model actually disagree.

What actually moved

  1. 01
    Jahmyr Gibbs is the single biggest disagreement in the dataset: JP $64 vs. a market-implied $120 — +$56. His own contract is genuinely, currently quoted at 21¢–43¢ (not a stub) — real money thinks he's a much clearer RB1 than our VORP curve credits him for.
  2. 02
    The market is more convicted on the very top of the WR pool across the board: Puka Nacua +$39, Jaxon Smith-Njigba +$34, Amon-Ra St. Brown +$24. Our model treats WR1–WR4 as a fairly even tier; the market sees real separation.
  3. 03
    Jonathan Taylor and Derrick Henry go the other way, hard: our #4 and #5 RBs by JP $ get priced by the market near the RB pool's floor — −$36 and −$25. Bettors are pricing in age/workload regression a season-total projection doesn't see coming.
  4. 04
    At QB, the market disagrees with the shape of our model, not just individual names: it spreads value further down the position (Josh Allen −$4, Jayden Daniels −$5) instead of concentrating it in the top three the way our scarcity math does.
⚠

Read the greyed-out rows as "no signal," not "tied." Below roughly the top 8–10 names per position, Polymarket's quoted price is just the midpoint of a nearly-empty order book (bid near $0.00, ask near $0.10) — nobody has actually traded there. Summed across a position, these phantom quotes push the field's total implied probability well past 100%, which means they're diluting the studs' true share rather than inflating it. Treat every dimmed row below as filler, and the bold rows above it as the real finding.

Method & caveats

How the reallocation works

Each Polymarket market is a field of "will X finish #1 in fantasy points at his position" contracts. Treated as a Plackett–Luce choice model, a player's normalized probability within the field is his relative strength — no de-vig math needed beyond normalizing named candidates to sum to 1. We then redistribute the same total JP $ our model already assigns to that position's matched players, in the market's proportions instead of VORP/scarcity. That's a fair apples-to-apples comparison of shape, not a claim about the market's absolute dollar value of the position.

Where this breaks down

  • These are "leads the league" markets, not yardage/points over-unders — inherently more top-heavy than an auction-value curve should be.
  • The named field's probabilities sum to well over 100% once thin/unseeded quotes are included — see the callout above. We don't correct for it here; a tighter cut (real bid required) would sharpen every finding above, not soften it.
  • JP $ here uses generic 12-team/$200/default-roster settings, not this league's actual saved settings.
  • Each market lists 111 candidates; most are named "Player 1"–"Player 60" or real players outside our own pool (backups, rookies) — dropped from the reallocation entirely.
